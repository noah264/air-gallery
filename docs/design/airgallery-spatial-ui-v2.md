# AirGallery UI concept v2

Generation mode: built-in image_gen, editing v1 as a visual reference.

Image: [airgallery-spatial-ui-v2.png](./airgallery-spatial-ui-v2.png)

## Design intent

- Homepage shows only photo groups on a dark backdrop; no persistent controls, text or camera preview.
- Each group displays its first photo as cover, with offset photographic sheets behind it to communicate multiple pages.
- Irregular scale, orientation, placement and depth replace the regular curved wall of v1.
- The image is a static concept. Group detection, within-group ordering and page transition animation remain to be discussed.
- Required setup, permission and error interfaces appear separately or on demand.

## Final generation prompt

Use case: ui-mockup. Redesign the attached AirGallery UI concept according to the user's new art direction. Keep beautiful photographic content and a sense of depth, but replace the entire composition.

Deliverable: ONE high-fidelity widescreen 16:10 desktop application HOME SCREEN, edge-to-edge flat screenshot. This home screen shows ONLY PHOTOGRAPHS on a near-black background, with no visible application chrome at all.

Primary visual idea: A chaotic yet art-directed three-dimensional field of PHOTO STACKS. The library consists of groups of images. Each group is one stack: its FIRST PHOTO is the clearly visible front cover, with 3 to 6 distinct photographic sheets behind it, offset and slightly rotated in a small fan, their fine pale paper edges and narrow strips of real photos clearly visible. These are loose stacked photographic prints, not books, not folders, not bound albums, no spirals. The multiple sheets behind each cover MUST unmistakably communicate that each group has more images/pages. Every visible group must have this layered stack construction; do not render isolated single cards.

Composition: About 11 to 14 irregularly scattered floating photo stacks, variable size, scale, angle, and depth, with generous irregular black negative space between clusters. Asymmetrical, slightly messy and organic, no grid, no rows, no circular arc, no carousel, no symmetrically centered hero. Some groups gently overlap, some small groups recede into the distance, and 2 or 3 groups are naturally cropped by screen edges to imply a larger spatial canvas. Keep most covers visible and recognizable. Large foreground group slightly left of center near bottom with alpine lake landscape; medium vertical desert-dune group in upper right; sea cliffs at upper left; Tokyo night, autumn forest, architecture, waterfall and coastal village covers scattered at diverse depths. Vary landscape and portrait ratios. Back sheets can peek in differing directions and have subtle believable thickness; stacks each contain internally related photos. Slight perspective, tasteful small rotations, visible front-to-back layering, realistic soft occlusion shadows. Imagine someone released groups of travel photos into a calm dark weightless space.

Visual styling: sophisticated minimal spatial photo browser, charcoal almost black backdrop, soft very subtle atmospheric depth, tactile photographic paper edges, accurate rectangular photograph geometry. Photographs have rich natural colors. Subtle light on paper edges makes groups readable. No bright neon or blue outlines, no heavy fog, no lens flare, no physical tabletop, no room or museum. This is a usable app's spatial canvas, not a promotional poster or hardware mockup.

Critical removals from reference: remove ALL branding, logo, headings, filenames, captions, directory chips, toolbar, icons, navigation dots, camera preview, hand tracking overlays, cursors, gesture hints, switches, progress indicators, page counts, window bars and text. ZERO typography, ZERO buttons, ZERO visible controls. Only beautiful irregular layered photo stacks in dark space. The homepage is the photos themselves.
